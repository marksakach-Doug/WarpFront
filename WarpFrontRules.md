# **WARP FRONT v4.0**

**A cooperative exact-sum race across a star map against the Borg**

1–3 players • about 20 minutes • one standard 52-card deck, jokers removed

---

## **1\. The Core Idea**

Three allied factions race to secure five planets before the Borg assimilates five. Warp cards are speed. The cards physically placed beside a planet show the speed committed there, so no paper tracking or counters are needed.

The human side must hit planets **exactly**. The Borg may overshoot. This creates the main tension: players solve exact-number puzzles while the Borg simply powers through them.

---

## **2\. Factions**

| Suit | Faction | Home Planets |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| ♥ Hearts | Klingons | J♥, Q♥, K♥ |
| ♣ Clubs | Ferengi | J♣, Q♣, K♣ |
| ♦ Diamonds | Federation | J♦, Q♦, K♦ |
| ♠ Spades | Borg | None |

The three player factions are identical in the core game except for which planets are their home worlds. (Optional faction abilities are in Section 12.)

**Home bonus:** a warp card played on a planet of its own suit is worth **\+1 speed**. This applies to every such card individually.

---

## **3\. Components and Setup**

1. **Separate the deck.** Each player faction uses A–10 of its suit as its 10-card warp deck. The nine J/Q/K of Hearts, Clubs, and Diamonds are planets. A–K of Spades form the 13-card Borg deck.  
2. **Lay out the planets.** Shuffle the nine planet cards and place them face-up in any arrangement you like. A 3×3 grid with a card-height gap between rows works well. Position has no effect on play. Jack \= 11, Queen \= 12, King \= 13\.  
3. **Assign the factions.** All three factions are always in play.  
   * 3 players: each player controls one faction.  
   * 2 players: one player controls two factions.  
   * Solo: one player controls all three.  
4. **Draw opening hands.** Each faction shuffles its warp deck and draws 3 cards. (Hand size is 3 unless the difficulty setting says otherwise.)  
5. **Set up discard piles.** Each faction has its **own** face-up discard pile beside its deck. The Borg has its own face-up Borg Discard. Discard piles may be examined at any time.  
6. **Prepare the Borg deck.** Shuffle the Spades and place them face-down. Turn the top card face-up. This is the **Sensor Contact** (see Section 7).  
7. **Choose a difficulty** (Section 11\) and agree on the table-talk rule (Section 10).

---

## **4\. How the Table Is Organized**

Whenever a planet is being contested, place player warp cards directly **below** it and Borg cards directly **above** it. Fan each side slightly so all ranks and suits remain visible.

Example: Borg cards above → ♠6 ♠3 / Q♥ / ♥5 ♣4 ♦2 ← player cards below

The planet card stays in the center. When it is resolved, sweep all cards beside it into the appropriate discard piles (each player card to its own faction's discard, each Borg card to the Borg Discard).

**Scanned cards** (Section 6A) sit face-up in front of their faction, not beside a planet.

---

## **5\. Round Structure**

Factions act in a fixed order, and the Borg acts immediately after each faction:

1. Federation (♥) acts.  
2. Borg acts.  
3. Klingons (♣) act.  
4. Borg acts.  
5. Vulcans (♦) act.  
6. Borg acts.

All three factions act every round, regardless of player count. The fixed order lets the team plan hand-offs (one faction sets up a planet, the next finishes it).

---

## **6\. Faction Turn**

### **A. Scan (optional)**

At the start of your turn, if you have no Scanned card, you may turn **one card in your hand face-up** in front of you. It stays face-up and public until you play it. A Scanned card is still part of your hand. You may not turn it back down. (The Federation ability in Section 12 allows two.)

### **B. Choose a target**

A faction may work on **one planet at a time**. In normal play the team will usually keep building the same planet until it is finished. A faction may switch targets, but all of that faction's cards at the old planet are discarded. Other factions' cards stay there.

### **C. Play exactly one warp card (or Regroup)**

Play one card from your hand beside the chosen planet. Aces count as 1; 2–10 count at face value. Add \+1 if the planet matches the card's suit.

A card may only be played if the total does **not** exceed the planet's distance (see D).

**Regroup.** If **no** card in your hand can legally be played on **any** active planet, you must Regroup instead of playing:

* Discard one card from your hand to your faction discard.  
* Then draw one card. If your deck is empty, first shuffle your discard pile (not including the card you just discarded) to form a new deck.  
* Your cards already beside a planet stay there. Your turn ends, and the Borg acts.

You may not Regroup if you have a legal play.

### **D. Check for arrival**

Add **all** player speed currently beside the planet. Players must hit the printed distance exactly.

Example: a Queen needs 12\. The players have 7 \+ 3 \= 10\. A 2 would claim it. A 4 would not; 10 \+ 4 \= 14, so that card cannot be played there.

If the total equals the planet's distance, the players claim it immediately. Turn the planet card **face-down**. Sweep all cards beside it into their discards.

If the total is below the distance, the planet remains active.

### **E. Draw back to hand size**

Draw until your faction has 3 cards. If your deck is empty, shuffle your faction discard to form a new deck and continue. Cards in hands or beside planets are not in the discard. If deck and discard are both empty, draw nothing.

---

## **7\. The Borg Turn**

### **A. Choose the target**

The Borg looks at every upright planet and chooses the one the players are threatening most: the planet with the greatest **total player speed** already committed to it.

* If tied, choose the tied planet with the **least remaining distance** (printed distance minus player speed).  
* If still tied, choose the **lowest printed distance**: Jack, then Queen, then King.  
* If still tied, the team chooses.

Note that if the table holds no player cards at all, every planet is tied at zero, so the Borg attacks a Jack.

### **B. Reveal a Borg card**

Take the face-up Sensor Contact card and place it **above** the chosen planet.

* **A–10:** Add its printed value as Borg speed. Aces count as 1\.  
* **J, Q, or K:** Count the card as **10** Borg speed, then reveal exactly one additional Borg card from the top of the deck and place it on the same planet. The bonus card adds its value normally; if it is a J/Q/K it simply counts as another 10 and causes no further reveal.

### **C. Check for assimilation**

Add **all** Borg speed above that planet. Unlike players, the Borg may go **over** the printed distance. If Borg speed is greater than or equal to the planet's distance, the Borg assimilates it immediately. Turn the planet card **sideways**. Sweep all cards beside it into their discards.

If the Borg has not reached the distance, the planet remains active and its Borg cards stay there. Borg cards persist until the planet is resolved, even if the players move away.

### **D. Sensor Contact**

After the Borg turn is fully resolved, turn the new top card of the Borg deck face-up. Everyone can see the next Borg card before deciding their turn.

A face-up J/Q/K means a **burst** is coming: 10 speed plus a hidden bonus card. A burst will always take a Jack, will very likely take a Queen or King, and lands on the players' leading planet (or on a Jack if the table is empty). If you can claim your leading planet this turn, do it.

### **E. Borg deck exhaustion**

The Borg deck is exhausted when the Borg must reveal a card and the deck is empty.

* **First time:** shuffle the Borg Discard (cards still sitting on planets are not included) to form a new deck, and continue the turn. Turn the new top card face-up at the end of the turn as usual.  
* **Second time:** the game ends immediately, even in the middle of a Borg turn (for example, when a bonus card is needed). Apply the end-of-game count in Section 9\.

If the discard is empty when the first exhaustion occurs, treat it as the second.

## **8\. Planet States**

| Orientation | Meaning |
| ----- | ----- |
| Face-up / upright | Active planet; still contested. |
| Face-down | Claimed by the Alliance. |
| Sideways | Assimilated by the Borg. |

The table itself records the score and the status of the sector.

---

## **9\. Winning and Losing**

* Players win immediately when they claim their **5th** planet.  
* The Borg wins immediately when it assimilates its **5th** planet.  
* If the Borg deck is exhausted a second time before either side reaches five, count the planets. Players win only if they have **strictly more** claimed planets than the Borg has assimilated; otherwise the Borg wins.

---

## **10\. Table Talk**

The team may discuss strategy freely. What you may say depends on the mode.

**Standard talk rule (hidden hands):**

* Keep your hand hidden from teammates.  
* You **may** discuss anything visible or strategic: planets, remaining distances ("we need 5 more"), Borg cards, the Sensor Contact, Scanned cards, and plans ("let's finish the Queen").  
* You **may not** state or hint at the rank or suit of any unscanned card in your hand. No "I have a 3," no "I can finish that," no "don't play a 4," and no meaningful pauses, groans, or nods.  
* Scanning is how you legitimately share information.

**Open-hand talk rule (Cadet and Solo):** hands are face-up and everything may be discussed.

In a 2-player game, the player controlling two factions knows both hands, and the talk rule applies between the two players.

---

## **11\. Difficulty and Player Counts**

The Borg's behavior does not change with player count; difficulty comes from information and setup.

| Level | Setup | Sim win rate\* |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Ensign | Standard, but hand size 4 | \~74% |
| **Standard** | Hidden hands, Section 10 talk rule, Scan | **\~55%** |
| Veteran | Standard, but no Scan | \~40% |
| Admiral | Standard, and the Borg starts with one planet already assimilated | \~26% |
| Cadet | Hands face-up (open-hand talk rule) | \~88% |
| **Solo** | All three hands visible to you, and the Borg starts with one planet assimilated | \~62% |

\*Approximate player win rate from about 3,000 simulated games with a simple greedy bot. Human teams reason better than the bot, so expect higher rates than shown, especially at Standard and above.

**Borg head start (Admiral and Solo):** after shuffling the nine planets, turn the **top card** sideways as assimilated, then lay out the remaining eight. The Borg now needs four more; the players still need five.

## **12\. Optional Faction Abilities**

These are **untested**. Add them once the base game feels comfortable.

* **Federation, Open Channels:** you may keep **two** Scanned cards at a time.  
* **Klingons, Battle Fury:** a Klingon card played on a planet that already has at least one Borg card is worth **\+1** (in addition to any home bonus).  
* **Vulcans, Logic:** you may look at the top card of your own warp deck at any time.

---

## **13\. Clarifications**

1. **Multiple factions on one planet:** allowed and normal. The combined total of all factions' cards must hit the distance exactly.  
2. **Home bonus values:** on a home planet your cards are worth 2 (Ace) to 11 (10). On any other planet they are worth 1 to 10\.  
3. **Remaining distance of exactly 1:** only a non-home Ace can finish it, and only two such Aces exist per game. If nobody can, the planet is a dead end. Abandon it by switching targets.  
4. **Switching targets** costs only the switching faction's own cards at the old planet. Borg cards there stay, so a planet the Borg has heavily loaded is dangerous to return to.  
5. **Borg cards on the table** stay visible above the planet. The Borg does not prefer planets it already has cards on; targeting looks only at player speed.  
6. **A face-card burst** places both cards and checks assimilation once, after the bonus card.  
7. **Player decks:** each faction has 10 cards, so a typical game rarely rebuilds a deck.  
8. **Regroup** is forced only when no legal play exists. It is not a voluntary pass.  
9. **End of game:** stop immediately when either side reaches five, or on the second Borg deck exhaustion.

---

## **14\. What Makes the Game Work**

The main player challenge is communication under a constraint. Everyone is constantly thinking about exact totals: "We need 9 on the Queen. Who can help?" Because players must hit the exact number, high cards are not automatically better. A card that overshoots is unusable on that target.

The Borg is a clock and a spike generator. Its deck holds exactly three face cards per pass, each a burst that can take a planet on its own. Sensor Contact lets the team see the spike coming, and the leading-planet targeting rule means every commitment you make is also a target. Scan is your only legal channel for information, so choose what to show and when.
